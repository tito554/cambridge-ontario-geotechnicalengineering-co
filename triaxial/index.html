The glacial geology under Cambridge tells a complicated story. Beneath the surface of Hespeler Road and the subdivisions spreading west toward the 401, you hit a mix of dense Halton Till overlying stratified silt and clay lenses from the ancient Lake Whittlesey basin. The Grand River cut through these deposits, leaving behind terraces where the near-surface soils can vary from stiff clay to loose silty sand within a single city block. When a triaxial test gets ordered here, it is usually because the standard SPT numbers are borderline and the geotechnical engineer needs to know whether that silt layer will drain during shear or build up pore pressure and fail. Our lab on the western edge of the GTA runs consolidated-undrained and unconsolidated-undrained triaxial tests under CSA A23.3 and ASTM D4767 protocols, giving you the effective stress parameters that make or break a deep foundation design in Cambridge’s variable overburden. Often we see projects where the CPT test logs show a tricky transition zone at 6 to 9 meters, and the triaxial data becomes the deciding factor for bearing capacity assumptions.

Triaxial testing in Cambridge glacial soils often reveals effective friction angles 3 to 5 degrees lower than empirical correlations predict – a difference that changes foundation dimensions.

Scope of work in Cambridge Ontario

A recent job over near the Toyota plant in the industrial park comes to mind. The borehole logs showed a stiff clay till that looked competent, but the project involved a heavily loaded mat foundation and the owner’s engineer wanted to be certain about long-term settlement. We ran a series of CIU triaxial tests with pore pressure measurement, and the effective friction angle came back at 27 degrees – lower than the undrained strength suggested. That discrepancy changed the foundation geometry. That is what triaxial testing does in Cambridge: it separates the apparent strength from the real strength when water pressure is accounted for. The equipment at our lab applies confining pressures up to 1,000 kPa, which covers most overburden stresses you will encounter across Waterloo Region. For granular materials from the Paris Moraine, we often pair triaxial testing with a grain size analysis to confirm the fines content, because even 10 percent silt can shift the drained behavior noticeably. On projects where the excavation goes below the water table, the CU triaxial with pore pressure readings becomes essential – and we have seen enough Cambridge basement digs to know that groundwater shows up earlier than the desktop study predicts.

Critical ground factors in Cambridge Ontario

The Ontario Building Code references CSA A23.3 for concrete structures, but the geotechnical input that feeds into those designs depends on shear strength parameters that default tables cannot provide for Cambridge’s glacial stratigraphy. The risk in this area is not that the soil is weak – Halton Till can be quite stiff – but that it is heterogeneous. A foundation designed on undrained shear strength alone may ignore the consolidation-driven loss of strength that occurs when a silty layer between till units starts to drain under long-term loading. The 2019 revision of the NBCC structural commentaries explicitly discusses the need for effective stress analysis in low-plasticity silts, which describes exactly the sort of material found along the Grand River floodplain. If the triaxial testing is skipped and the designer uses textbook values for “glacial till,” the factor of safety on bearing capacity can erode from 3.0 to below 1.5 without any visible warning. We have seen this play out in slope stability assessments along the river valley walls, where CU triaxial data made the difference between a stable cut and a recommended setback increase of 4 meters.

Our services

Our Cambridge triaxial testing program covers the full range of shear strength characterization needed for regional geotechnical practice. Each test is run on a calibrated frame with automated data acquisition, and the reports include Mohr-Coulomb envelopes ready for direct input into finite element or limit equilibrium models.

CIU Triaxial (ASTM D4767)

Consolidated-undrained test with pore pressure measurement. Provides effective stress parameters c’ and φ’ for long-term stability analysis in Cambridge silty clays and tills.

UU Triaxial (ASTM D2850)

Unconsolidated-undrained quick test for total stress parameters. Used for short-term bearing capacity checks during construction, especially in low-permeability Halton Till.

Multi-Stage Triaxial

Single-specimen testing at increasing confining pressures. Cost-effective for projects with limited sample recovery, common in the gravelly till zones west of Highway 24.

Triaxial with Bender Elements

Shear wave velocity measurement during triaxial shearing. Links small-strain stiffness to strength, useful when pairing with MASW surface wave data for seismic site class determination.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a triaxial test cost in Cambridge, Ontario?

A standard CIU triaxial test with pore pressure measurement runs between $2,790 and $3,450 CAD depending on the number of confining stages and whether you need a full report with Mohr-Coulomb interpretation. Multi-stage tests on a single specimen fall at the lower end of that range.

When should I order a CU triaxial versus a UU triaxial?

Order a UU (unconsolidated-undrained) test when you need short-term bearing capacity for a foundation on low-permeability soil like the Halton Till in Cambridge, where drainage during construction is negligible. Order a CU (consolidated-undrained) test with pore pressure measurement when you need effective stress parameters for long-term settlement or slope stability analysis – which is most projects involving permanent structures.

What sample quality do you need for a reliable triaxial test?

We need undisturbed Shelby tube samples, preferably 75 mm diameter, with no visible disturbance, drying, or freezing. The thin-walled tubes must be sealed immediately after extrusion in the field. For the silty layers common in Cambridge beneath the till, sample disturbance shows up as softened strength values, so we always check the B-value during saturation to confirm the specimen has not been compromised.

How long does it take to get triaxial test results?

A standard CU triaxial test with three confining stages takes about 7 to 10 working days from sample receipt to final report. The consolidation phase alone requires 24 to 48 hours depending on soil permeability, and the shearing stage must be slow enough to allow pore pressure equalization. UU tests are faster – usually 3 to 5 working days.
